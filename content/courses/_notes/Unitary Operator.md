They preserve the inner product and, consequently, the probabilities of quantum states. It satisfies the condition: $\hat{U} \hat{U}^\dagger = \hat{U}^\dagger \hat{U} = 1$

Also, the inverse of the unitary operator is equal to its [[Hermitian Operator|Hermitian]] adjoint: $\quad U^{-1} = U^\dagger$

The matrix form of a unitary operator and adjoint shows:
$$\hat{U} \hat{U}^\dagger = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a^* & c^* \\ b^* & d^* \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

For it to be unitary, the following conditions also must hold:
$a a^* + b b^* = |a|^2 + |b|^2 = 1, \quad c c^* + d d^* = |c|^2 + |d|^2 = 1, \quad a c^* + b d^* = 0$
