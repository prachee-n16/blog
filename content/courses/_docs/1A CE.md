> [!note] 
> I’m writing these reflections about five years after taking these courses, with the benefit of hindsight and a lot more perspective. So, a lot of these details are fuzzy but the overall impressions for how each course felt at the time have stuck with me. I hope this perspective is helpful for underclassmen who are trying to make sense of what lies ahead.

This term was a *hybrid* term; It was the first term in-person classes were being held under COVID restrictions. O-Week was held online for us, larger lectures remained virtual, and only smaller tutorials were conducted in person. Overall, the term felt transitional and somewhat isolating, as everyone was still figuring out how things worked, which added an extra challenge to starting university.

## ARTS 190 - First-Year Topics in Arts
This course involved a *lot of writing* and was mostly an introduction to technical writing. Note, the experience can vary significantly depending on the professor. In our case, we were assigned to write a paper for the ISTAS conference and understanding the full academic writing process. You choose a research topic, find your thesis & arguments, submit draft proposals and getting familiar with IEEE citation styles. I wrote mine on AI governance, and despite the heavy workload, it's a pretty bird course! 

## ECE 105 - Classical Mechanics
This was easily the hardest course of the term, like the one that every first-year ends up trauma bonding over for years to come. The course covers a wide range of physics topics, including kinematics, dynamics and circular motion, work and energy, oscillations, and wave motion. If you want to pass this course, attending lectures alone is not enough. You really have to grind through the assigned problem sets, and if you have time, do even more problems on your own. Keep solving problems until you feel you have the intuition to solve questions on a first glance. Having a background in IB HL Physics definitely helped, but the problems you’ll see here are still significantly harder.

## ECE 150 - Fundamentals of Programming
This course had students with all kinds of coding backgrounds, from complete beginners to very experienced programmers. So, the course starts off pretty slow where you spend a lot of time on basics like arrays, sorting, and other fundamentals. After the midterm though, the pace ramps up fast where within a month, we cover linked lists, OOP, and recursion. I highly recommend starting the projects as soon as they come out and practicing writing C++ code on paper without any kind of IDE. 

## ECE 190 - Engineering Profession and Practice
This course is meant to get you thinking about engineering design decisions and ethics. Every class, you’re given a prompt and work in a group to come up with a response. These responses are submitted to Kritik where students review and evaluate each other’s work anonymously. It's a pretty clear bird course and will most likely end up being the highest grade of the term. 

## ECE 198 - Engineering Project Studio
This is honestly a pretty fun course. You get to choose a project idea that can be built using a starter kit, and from there, the level of creativity is entirely up to you. There are some really cool projects people come up with, but how ambitious you go depends on how much time you want to invest.

We chose a fairly simple project - a Simon Says game because, honestly, the first term is hard and time is limited. That said, I’ve seen others build things like hardware versions of Dance Dance Revolution, Morse code translators, and more. You’ll be working with an STM32 development board (keep it, since it’s used in later projects), and any additional materials depend on your project. We bought a starter hardware kit with LEDs, resistors, and other components, which ended up being more than enough.

There’s also a fair amount of report writing involved, mainly focused on documenting your technical decisions for the project.

## MATH 115 - Linear Algebra for Engineering
This is an introductory linear algebra course, and a big part of it is problem grinding to get comfortable with the math. If you’re able to visualize what the problems are actually doing, especially with vectors and transformations, you’ll be in a much better spot. There’s also a lot of great YouTube content out there that can really help build intuition, especially if the lectures feel a bit abstract at first.

## MATH 117 - Calculus I for Engineering
This is an introductory calculus course, and it’s one of the most important ones to get your fundamentals right in. Don’t procrastinate on this course, and don’t try to cut corners. You really want to grind these concepts into your bones, because you’ll be using them constantly throughout your degree. This is also one of the only times you’ll get to take things slow and truly learn the material in depth. If you’ve taken IB HL Math, congrats - you’ll already be familiar with a lot of the content around derivatives and integration, which gives you a nice head start.
