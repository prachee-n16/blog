This term was a rollercoaster. Summers at Waterloo are honestly amazing - the weather pulls you out of that winter depression, and it’s hard not to want to spend all your time outside. Unfortunately, the course load that term was… challenging, to say the least.

This was also the first term where you really get a taste of what I’d consider the _real_ foundations of Computer Engineering. The courses start diving into the core concepts and technical basics that the rest of the degree builds on, which makes the term both **important** and **demanding**.

## ECE 106 - Electricity and Magnetism
The sister of [[1A CE#ECE 105 - Classical Mechanics|ECE 105]] and this was THE hardest course of the term. It’s conceptually difficult, and the problems move well beyond plug-and-chug templates. Instead, you’re encouraged to develop a deeper understanding of how to choose the right problem representation, whether that’s selecting an appropriate coordinate system or identifying the correct surface. Even with a solid grasp of the theory, many problems still need strong intuition like a a clever application of Gauss’s Law or a non-obvious integral setup that could make or break the solution. The questions often felt more like puzzles than routine physics problems, where one wrong assumption, sign, or direction would mess you up. 

That said, Prof. Saini was amazing. I still remember his office hours running late into the evening for panicked students, patiently walking through problems and breaking them down with really clear explanations. Interestingly, the course has since been reduced in difficulty for lower years.
